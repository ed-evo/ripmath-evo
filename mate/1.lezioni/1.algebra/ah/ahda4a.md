Risolviamo l'equazione
$$
\textcolor{red}{y^2 + 3y - 40 = 0}
$$
applichiamo la formula

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}}
$$

Abbiamo:
$\textcolor{blue}{a = 1}$
$\textcolor{blue}{b = 3}$
$\textcolor{blue}{c = -40}$

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{3^2 - 4(1)(-40)}}{2}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 160}}{2}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{169}}{2}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-3 \pm 13}{2}}
$$

Ora prendo una volta il meno e una volta il più

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_1 = \frac{-3 - 13}{2} = -8}
$$

$$
\textcolor{blue}{y_2 = \frac{-3 + 13}{2} = 5}
$$

Ho quindi le soluzioni
$$
\textcolor{red}{y_1 = -8} \quad \textcolor{red}{y_2 = 5}
$$
