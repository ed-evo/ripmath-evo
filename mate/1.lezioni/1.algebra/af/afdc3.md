# esercizio

$$\textcolor{blue}{x^4 + 13x^2 + 36 = 0}$$ pongo

$$\textcolor{red}{x^2 = y}$$ e quindi $$\textcolor{red}{x^4 = y^2}$$

$$
\textcolor{red}{y^2 + 13y + 36 = 0}
$$

risolvo rispetto ad $$y$$

$$
\textcolor{red}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{red}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{169 - 144}}{2}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{red}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2}}
$$

$$
\textcolor{red}{y_{1,2} = \frac{-13 \pm 5}{2}}
$$

- $$\textcolor{red}{y_1 = (-13 + 5)/2 = -8/2 = -4}$$
- $$\textcolor{red}{y_2 = (-13 - 5)/2 = -18/2 = -9}$$

Ora devo risolvere le due equazioni:

- $$\textcolor{red}{x^2 = -9}$$
- $$\textcolor{red}{x^2 = -4}$$

sono equazioni a [valori immaginari](../an/ana.html)

- risolvo la prima $$\textcolor{red}{x = \pm\sqrt{-9}}$$
    - $$x_1 = -3i$$
    - $$x_2 = +3i$$
- risolvo la seconda $$\textcolor{red}{x = \pm\sqrt{-4}}$$
    - $$x_3 = -2i$$
    - $$x_4 = +2i$$

quindi ho 4 soluzioni immaginarie (metto in fila):

$$
\textcolor{blue}{x_1 = -3i \quad x_2 = -2i \quad x_3 = +2i \quad x_4 = +3i}
$$
